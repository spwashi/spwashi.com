Local Proof Cards

Screenshot-native records for asks, offers, pledges, references, and decisions. Read a card, or scroll the rooms.

A Local Proof Card is a small, readable record you can keep. It can describe what someone needs, what someone offers, what a group agreed to, what a project is asking for, or what insight should be remembered. It is also a way to catch the work while it is still becoming itself.

The card stays useful as a screenshot, a copied seed, a link, or a later archived record. No wallet required. No platform lock-in required. The first version is local, readable, and shareable. The screenshot matters because it freezes a state without pretending the process is over.

Use this when

a claim, ask, offer, scene note, funding request, or learning milestone needs a durable handle.

What it protects

context, authorship, sequence, and the difference between “someone said this” and “this is true.”

What it can become

a screenshot, prompt packet, membership artifact, consensus record, RPG recap seed, or research citation. It can also become a prompt for the next pass through the work.

Semantic anchors and references

Cards stay useful when nearby routes give them structure, theory, and a next action. These pages explain how to extend the record into spells, prompts, route anchors, or research notes without losing the object. The card is the visible state; the routes are the continuation.

?definition

What A Card Does

A card makes a claim. A screenshot preserves it. A URL or seed lets someone revisit it. A later version can supersede it. Between those states is where a lot of useful understanding happens.

These are NFT-like in the narrow sense that each card is a distinct object with its own context. They are not speculative assets. They are local records first.

?safety

Keep The Record Honest

A card is not proof that a claim is true. It is proof that a claim was made in a readable form at a specific moment.

If a card changes, make a new version. Do not silently overwrite the record.

#>consensus_flow

Screenshot To Consensus

Consensus does not mean everyone agrees forever. It means the group has a visible reference point for what is being proposed, supported, questioned, blocked, revised, or archived.

Draft

A proposal exists. No agreement yet.

Supported

Enough people support moving forward.

Concerned

People agree partly but have notes.

Blocked

Someone names a concrete reason not to proceed.

Revised

A new version replaces the old one.

Archived

No longer active, but preserved.

Screenshot the card before the conversation spreads out. The card gives the group one thing to point at.

Where cards connect

Cards are useful because they turn attention into a stable object that other routes can reuse.

  • CoordinationUse cards to stabilize roles, proposals, and consensus.
  • MembershipTurn interest into a readable role, offer, or support path.
  • ResearchPromote notes into evidence, source language, and decisions.
  • RPG sessionsUse recap cards to protect table memory before lore hardens.
