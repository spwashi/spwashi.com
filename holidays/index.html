A holiday is a day a group agrees to notice.

Celebration is how a group says out loud what mattered. A holiday season is a good time to wonder what we are making and how we are growing.

The table gets set, someone raises a glass, and a thing that happened all year finally gets a name. That agreement is the meaning. The food and the gifts carry it, and the carrying is worth doing because everyone at the table decided, together, that today counts.

Come for the ideas, bring a team to celebrate, or read it on the way to something else; the questions work at any of those distances.

From Octvember to the new year

The season has four turns, and each one asks a different question about the work and the people doing it.

Octvember: costuming

A costume can be an affirmation: a way of saying yes to someone you are becoming. It is the easiest time of year to make something you would not sign.

Ask: what would we make if nobody knew it was ours?

The costume sheet keeps what the night held, and what stayed.

The long table

A shared meal is a receipt for a year of help: every dish arrived because someone carried it. Gratitude is how the table reads the receipt aloud.

Ask: who made this year possible, and have we told them?

The gift season

A gift is a guess about what someone values, wrapped so the guess can be a surprise. The best ones prove somebody was listening.

Ask: what did we make this year that someone would want to keep?

The threshold

The year turns and time feels writable again. New Year Joy keeps the vessels for what survives the midnight feeling.

Ask: what do we carry forward, and what do we set down?

#>season_boundary

A holiday is a season boundary

Work that grows all year needs a few days when the growth is said out loud instead of absorbed. Without them, everything changes a little and nothing is ever finished enough to celebrate.

A holiday gives a group that boundary for free. Name what was made. Say what changed and who changed it. Decide what the next season is for. This site keeps the same habit on a smaller clock: each release closes a cycle and names what it did before the next one opens.

?"questions_to_carry"

Questions to carry to the table

Bring one to a team dinner, a family call, or a quiet walk. None of them needs an answer tonight.

  • What did we make this year that we would make again?
  • What can we do together now that none of us could do alone in January?
  • Which small thing would we celebrate if we had noticed it?
  • Who should be at the table next year?
  • What are we ready to stop carrying?
