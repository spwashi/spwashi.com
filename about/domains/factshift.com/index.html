factshift.com

factshift.com began as an attempt at a better Wikipedia. Building it showed me I didn't know enough about how people learn to improve on what Wikipedia already does well, so the project turned toward learning itself. It is now a meditation in compressed form: short-form claims, shifts in framing, and small public probes. In 2019, the named project became lowercase to me as I explored phenomena through my relationship to code while developing the parser for Spw. Visitors can practice describing the experience too: say what the surface is doing, then compare the description with the thing itself.

One claim, from more than one place

An encyclopedia entry is written from one place, for everyone. What I wanted to write was a claim with its place in it: who is looking, from where, and what is still open. Spw grew from that want, as a way to write information with more than one dimension, seen from more than one perspective.

A line like ecestudent@ece.120~?[transistors work.how] reads: a student, situated in ECE 120 (@), holding open (~) a question (?) about how transistors work. The interface on factshift.com was prototyped in a burst over November and December 2023 and refined through 2024, until it settled with its v0.0.2-alpha release that October. factshift.center, new this year, carries that codebase into a more experimental domain. There the line becomes a canvas: the student, the course and the question are nodes to move, link and rewrite, and the line follows each change.

Role

Meditation on how phenomena become describable, using experimental writing and idea-compression.

Relation

Lives closer to snippets, probes, and small conceptual movements than to long-form worldbuilding. The public app makes the experience inspectable, cloneable, and extendable.
