Design / Folio Archive

Small worlds, held in a folio.

I make laminated folios as a meditative practice. Collage and laminate what is current; paint a season across the stack; then paint those same surfaces again the next year. Thirty-one are scanned now, so you can look closer and ask for a piece by card.

The stack is the studio, and the archive is an original canon: a few years of wonder kept on laminated sheets, continuous and mine, overlapping no one else's work. The scans come first below; the seed studies after them are a place to try ways of looking while the rest of the stack is still being painted.

A doodle from RPG Wednesday can become a table object or a story-led piece. Name the scene on the order card. A Seed is a small issue; a custom is quoted. Your mark is not automatically my inventory.

{studio_practice}

A stack I return to

A folio is not a one-sitting picture. It is a surface I can restack, so the same object can carry this year’s collage, this year’s laminate, and next year’s paint.

Collage, current

I collage what is in the room now: scraps, prints, marks, stills from play. Printer paper keeps the piece ordinary enough to handle and to stack.

Lamination, current

Lamination closes a layer so I can keep working without destroying it. The folio becomes a durable page in a stack I can pick up again.

Paint this year, paint next year

I paint on the laminated surface, then return a year later and paint again. The earlier season stays in the object; the new marks have to meet it.

Paint seasons

A trend of color or gesture can run across several folios in the same season, so the stack reads as a year, not only as isolated pictures.

Stackable materials

Tempera already kept layers from welding the stack together. Matte varnish is going on the acrylic so those surfaces can restack without sticking to themselves.

Review, since 2024

I have been iterating on the process and its review since 2024. Family members read the folios as they develop: people who work for school boards, former teachers, licensed counselors, and early childhood specialists. Their questions about who a page is for, and whether it would hold up in a room with children or families, change what I paint next.

Where their fields meet the rest of the site: teaching, counseling, human development.

What the return produces

A process I can trust, pictures that come from the stack instead of a blank sheet, and social posts that are receipts of the work. Orders are open by card; formats and working prices live below.

#>collect_a_folio

From my worktable to yours.

One painted surface can have several lives. I’m developing a minting process that gives each source folio a record, then traces the originals, prints, and derivative collages released from it.

Orders are open by card. Fill the order card below, then send a screenshot, the copied seed, or the downloaded text; I reply with the piece, its price from the working ranges, and shipping once I know where it goes. Minting means documenting and releasing a piece: its identity, physical form, provenance, and connection to the world.

The original

The painted original on standard printer paper, protected by lamination, with its accumulated marks and material history. Some originals may be offered; others may stay in my archive.

Its record will distinguish the physical original from any prints or collages made from its scan.

A print

A reproduction made from the scanned folio. Each release will specify its dimensions, paper, finish, and whether it is an open or numbered edition.

A way to take the image home while the original continues its own story.

A derivative collage

A new composition made from fragments of one or more folios. I’m also open to custom collages. Each piece’s record will link back to its sources and identify whether the offered piece is handmade or printed.

The lineage stays visible even when the image becomes something new.

Art from a story still unfolding

The folio canon is my own and continuous; lore.land is a second source for new pieces, not the origin of the archive. I also make new art from the developing stories of lore.land—a pantry for homebrew RPG campaigns. Characters, places, motifs, and scenes become ingredients for play, and sometimes become a new artwork, print, or collage.

Some TikTok group chats with streaks take part in RPG Wednesday — 314 days on September 17, the pet in outfits — making art and telling stories around the canon on lore.land. My own art grows alongside that shared play. A small, otherwise inconsequential wonder is enough to share.

For a custom collage or a story-led piece, bring me the scene or motif you have in mind — a Wednesday doodle is enough of a brief. We agree on format, scope, and price before I begin. A Seed original is the periodical door; Landmark and Relic are the objects that can sit on a show table.

What travels with a minted piece?

A source ID, an image of the work, dimensions and materials, known painting dates and later changes, and a short provenance note. Where a piece belongs to a lore.land chapter or appears in a public process record, its record can lead you there.

Every offer will name the exact object or edition, what is included, its price, and delivery details. Owning an original and owning a numbered print will remain distinct.

How to Read a Folio

A folio becomes more useful when it can be read through several lenses. The same object can hold visual pleasure, design judgment, artifact evidence, and community memory.

Art

Composition, figure-ground relationship, material selection, and physical gesture.

Design

Information hierarchy, color palette, edition logic, and surface behavior.

Skill

Practice prompts, technical constraints, and professional development value as a training surface.

Community

Recognition, relationships, circulation history, and shared creative context representing community signal.

~processing_loop

How a folio becomes a release

The studio practice feeds this loop; this loop is how a finished layer becomes a release. Capture the source once, then give every new form its own identity. A new collage can grow from an old folio without losing where it came from.

1. Scan the folio

Keep a full-resolution master of the front and back. Check the laminate for glare, and photograph a detail that shows the physical surface.

2. Give it a record

Assign a source ID. Record dimensions, materials, known dates, motifs, and the marks that make this object itself.

3. Choose its next form

Keep or offer the original, prepare a print, or compose a derivative collage. Link each new work to its source folio or folios.

4. Prepare the edition

Inspect the physical piece or print proof. Set its format, edition size if limited, price, and what accompanies it.

5. Release a small collection

Bring related pieces together with a name, provenance notes, and clear availability. Give each offer its own record.

6. Keep the lineage alive

Mark sold or retired offers while keeping the source record visible. New prints and collages become new branches of the same history.

#training_value

Training Value

A folio is useful when it teaches product judgment. Composition teaches hierarchy. Texture teaches material constraint. Repetition teaches system design. A finished object teaches packaging. A shared object teaches trust, context, and distribution.

~community_value

Community and Brand Value

The archive carries accumulated signal: recognizable visual language, repeated durable practice, community circulation, and relationships formed through shared creative space.

That momentum makes each artifact easier to explain, place, teach, commission, reference, and extend.
