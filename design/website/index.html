Website

This route is the cleaner design-facing entry for the website as a publishing surface. The point is not only that a website exists, but that it can act as a readable instrument: visible thresholds, inspectable structure, deliberate navigation, and enough interaction to teach the surface without overwhelming it.

The deeper system write-up still lives at the website field guide. This page is the shorter route for design categorization and for linking into the neighboring interaction and accessibility surfaces.

Questions the sites ask each other

Three sites now share one language. This one reads and publishes. spw.quest teaches Spw and takes a line apart. factshift.center lays a line out as a canvas you can move. A line can travel between them, and each move raises a product question that does not have a settled answer yet. These are the ones still open.

What travels with a line

  • When a line leaves one site for another, what should go with it: the line alone, its name, or the view of the canvas it should open into?
  • A short line can ride in the address, where anyone can read it; a named one rides encoded. Is a link you can read worth more than a link that remembers what it was called?
  • Where should a carried line's name come from: the place it sat on the page, a label a reader gave it, or its own first word?
  • Should every door to another site work with scripts off, even when that puts one more chip on a busy page?

Which reader sets the standard

  • The canvas and the guide each read Spw, and they do not always agree: a body inside a body, a scene written before its word. Which one is the reference, and how should a disagreement be shown instead of smoothed over?
  • A bench of test lines can press one feature at a time against the canvas. What belongs on a bench that a newcomer can also learn from?

Popular, for a language with no visitor counts

  • Without tracking, "popular" can mean what the sites have been writing lately. Is that a fair signal, or should a reader's own choices (what they carry, what they come back to) count, kept on their device?
  • Which pages should a search engine see: the stable rooms only, or every line as an address of its own?

Moving between ideas

  • When stepping between ideas is the browser's own back and forward, which moves deserve a step (a new idea) and which are only edits to the one in hand?
  • The edges of a screen move you, a selection names the thing, and an edit changes it. Does that one rule hold on every site, on a phone and with a keyboard?

What each site is for

  • Installed side by side, each site needs a job a person can name in a few words. Where the jobs overlap, is the overlap a door or a duplicate?
  • Someone arriving to hire and someone arriving to learn may want different first doors. Should the sites route them, or let both walk the same path and notice different things?
