01 · Kitchen & Ratio

A beet stains the board; a sauce remembers everything

Boiling water off a pan concentrates aroma and salt at different emotional speeds: what tasted timid at twenty minutes turns brassy at twenty-four. Season for the volume you are landing on, not the pot you started with.

Reduction is lossy state compression with a conserved invariant: volatile aromatics escape, water evaporates, but dissolved NaCl stays fixed in the buffer. Early salt scales inversely with final volume.

Treat seasoning like late-session stakes at the Wednesday table: hold the salt back while the scene is still boiling down, then commit once the room's final volume is clear.

Iron-gall ink, glaze tests, and stock reductions share one studio law — pigment and salt never leave the vessel when the carrier evaporates.

03 · Language & Table

A good notebook margin holds two kinds of ink: open brackets for questions still pulling at you, and closed lines for receipts that survived contact with the bench.

Spw models curiosity as a signed gradient: ~ holds uncollapsed potential, ~#expect commits a prediction, ! runs the probe, and * precipitates a receipt while ? keeps the outer horizon open.
