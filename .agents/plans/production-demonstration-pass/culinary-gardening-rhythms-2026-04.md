## Intent
Create a small family of images and short copy that make *plausible mathematical rhythms* feel magical and accessible across three overlapping audiences:
- **Gardeners** who want to see wonder and "magic" in stubborn or difficult crops.
- **Chefs** who find beauty and meaning in the repetitive, sometimes tedious precision required for great results.
- **Parents** who want simple, reliable bases that can be quietly enriched with imagination for children.

The work deliberately bridges recipes/ (fermentation, mise-en-place, reduction), topics/math/ (rhythms, symmetry, waves, Fibonacci), craft, and care surfaces. It uses the site's existing semantic vocabulary (valences, phases, operators, liminality) so the assets can become first-class citizens in the linguistic architecture.

## Cross-Concept Storytelling & Linking Architecture

These five images + the animation form a small constellation that can travel together or be used individually:

- **Rhythm as common language**: Bubbles, knife cuts, plant veins, dough layers, and pollinator paths are all forms of repetition that accumulate into something larger and more intelligent than any single action.
- **Natural bridges**:
  - Fermentation Bubble Choir ↔ Dough Tide Fold (slow living systems)
  - Pollination Golden Spiral ↔ Brassica Twilight (visible vs. hidden order in the garden)
  - Knife-Rhythm Lattice ↔ Dough Tide Fold (parallel grammars of repetition and accumulation)
- **Audience entry points**:
  - Gardeners enter through Brassica or Pollination.
  - Chefs enter through Knife or Fermentation (and stay for the Dough).
  - Parents and storytellers enter through Dough and are gently led toward the others.

All of them support the larger claim the site already makes: that careful, repeated attention to material (soil, dough, brine, a cutting board) is a form of mathematics that does not require notation to be true.
