Comprehension, Retrieval, and Limits

Educational psychology studies how people learn, remember, and stay motivated. Three results shape this site. Kintsch’s construction-integration model says a reader builds meaning from the text and then weaves it into what they already know. The testing effect says pulling an idea back out teaches more than reading it again. And working memory is bounded: a handful of chunks at a time. Spw began in a course on learning from text, and I ran cognitive assessment batteries for The Adult Learning Lab.

Principles

Construction-integration

Text becomes propositions, then a situation model joined to prior knowledge. Parsers do the first half for machines.

wiki: Walter Kintsch

The testing effect

Retrieval practice beats rereading for long-term memory, which is why the curriculum asks before it tells.

wiki: testing effect

A bounded working memory

People hold a few chunks at once; good design spends them carefully. See performance for the machine version of a budget.

Learning and thinking

Sankofa

An Akan principle from Ghana, often drawn as a bird reaching back: go back and fetch what was left behind. Retrieval, as a cultural practice.

wiki: Sankofa

At the table

The testing effect is a dough you have to make again. Rereading the card does not build the memory; cooking it on three different days does. Analects 2.11 says the same thing as a curriculum: review the old, and the new appears. The written card and the hands can disagree. That disagreement is a scene on RPG Wednesday, and the practice lives on living cultures.

Scene: the recipe card says one thing. The person who has cooked it says another. The table has to decide which memory it will stake a meal on.
