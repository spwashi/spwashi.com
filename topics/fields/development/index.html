Human, Software, Spiritual

Development is one word used in three places. Erikson describes each stage of a life as a crisis to work through, not a box to tick. Software keeps a version you can return to, so a mistake does not erase what came before. Fowler describes stages of faith as ways a person makes meaning across a lifetime. Read through each other they teach a few shared things: setbacks are part of growth, a scaffold comes down when it is no longer needed, and a life is not a version number.

#>parallel_theories

Parallel and Competing Theories

Stage models from China and India, older than the Western ones above, that end somewhere different: in ordinary life, or in letting go.

The Ten Ox-Herding Pictures

A twelfth-century Chinese Chan sequence in which a herder searches for, tames, and finally forgets the ox, then returns to the marketplace: development that ends in ordinary life.

wiki: Ten Bulls

The four āśramas

A classical Indian model of a life in four stages: student, householder, forest dweller, and renunciate, each with its own duties.

wiki: āśrama

At the table

The householder stage is the one that feeds people. The ox-herding pictures end in the market, not on the mountain: you return carrying something to share. A life still cannot be bisected like a commit. What can be played is the return. On RPG Wednesday, meal -> relation is that last picture. The kitchen version this week is the seasonal pot.
