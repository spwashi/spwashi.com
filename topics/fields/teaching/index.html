Design Backward, Check Often, Know the District

Teachers turn curriculum into a day that works for thirty different people. Backward design starts from what students should understand and works back to the lessons. Formative evidence checks often and changes the next lesson because of it. And a school board decides what a district actually makes available: the books, hours, and people behind every plan. Former teachers and people who work for school boards are among the family members who have reviewed my laminated folios since I began iterating on the process in 2024.

#>parallel_theories

Parallel and Competing Theories

Competing answers to what a school is for: a Brazilian challenge to lecture-and-memorize teaching, and schools built for distance and for freedom in Australia and India.

Freire’s critical pedagogy

Paulo Freire, teaching adult literacy in Brazil, argued against the "banking" model in which teachers deposit facts into students, and for learning through dialogue about the learners’ own world.

wiki: Paulo Freire

School of the Air

Since 1951, children on remote Australian stations have attended class by two-way radio, now by internet: distance education as public infrastructure.

wiki: School of the Air

Tagore’s Santiniketan

Rabindranath Tagore opened an open-air school in Bengal in 1901, later Visva-Bharati University, teaching through nature, art, and freedom rather than rote.

wiki: Visva-Bharati

At the table

Two curricula are already meals. Japanese kyūshoku has students portion, serve, and clear; the lesson is the lunch, not the break around it. The milpa, corn and beans and squash, is an agricultural curriculum: pole, climb, shade. Cook it as market succotash. On RPG Wednesday the scene is the portion: the party plates for everyone before anyone eats, and the argument is who gets what.
