Many Numbers at Once

Linear algebra handles many numbers at once. A vector is a list of numbers with a direction and a size; a matrix is a transformation that moves every vector in the same consistent way; solving a system of equations asks which input a transformation sends to a given output. Most of data science, computer graphics, and machine learning is linear algebra wearing different clothes.

Parallel and Competing Theories

The European names on these methods came late. Solving many equations at once was worked out in China first.

The Nine Chapters

The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art, compiled in China by around the first century CE, solves systems of linear equations by arranging coefficients in a grid and eliminating, the method later named for Gauss.

wiki: The Nine Chapters

At the table

The Nine Chapters teach linear systems on grain: millet, taxes, and how many units each cart should carry. The matrix is a ledger of food. Gaussian elimination is the later European name for a method the chapters already use. Cook the same three-crop constraint as market succotash, and play the ledger on RPG Wednesday as who gets which sack.

Scene: three grains, several carts, one fair division. The argument is the ledger, and someone is waiting to eat.
