Why forgetting full turns reveals hidden grain

Real-number division smooths everything out (14 ÷ 12 = 1.166…). Integer arithmetic refuses to forget the remainder (14 ≡ 2 mod 12). That productive act of throwing away full turns turns an infinite line into a finite wheel where prime bottlenecks, cyclic orbits, and shared factors become visible geometry.

Prime bottlenecks

Primes cannot be factored into smaller steps, so on a prime clock every non-zero multiplier permutes all hours cleanly without collisions.

Visible folding

When gcd(k, n) = d > 1, the wheel folds d-to-1 onto a smaller sub-polygon — divisibility drawn as chord density.

Field bridge

Because prime wheels never fold to zero, every non-zero residue has a multiplicative inverse — turning clock arithmetic into a full field.
