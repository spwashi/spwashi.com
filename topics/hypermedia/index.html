Hypermedia

I'm Spwashi. I build software and make art. Hypermedia, here, is a surface you can re-read, a capability the page offered, and a receipt another reader can check. Spw is the grammar I use for those three parts.

#>surface

Surface

The page is the medium. Headings, links, and the sentence you are reading have to hold without a script. Attributes such as data-spw-semantic-expression sit on that same document. They name a relation. They do not replace the sentence.

A host can be this site or another site that keeps the same habit: HTML first, a readable source beside it, and a projection that a person can open. The expression medium[surface]{document.reread} is the claim. It does not require a particular product underneath.

>capability

Capability

A link is a move this page offered. Following it enters another object with its own boundary: another section, another route, another host. The sigil on a chip says what kind of move it is. Wonder opens a probe. Action commits. A concept-edge projects onto another surface. A frame addresses a named handle.

Scholarship already asked for this at a larger scale. Alan Kay argued that a browser should safely run encapsulated objects fetched from the network, so content stays open instead of fitting a fixed menu. Ted Nelson’s hypermedia was a literature of connection: a passage that remains the same passage in more than one place, and links that can point both ways. I use those arguments as theory. I do not treat a neighboring implementation as something this page can speak for.

!receipt

Receipt

A receipt names what entered, what changed, and what a later reader can check. On this site that check is often by hand: the same seed rendered twice, or a claim with its witness still attached. Consequence arcs are the same shape in mathematics: state, constraint, response, receipt.

Interaction should leave that kind of trace. A tap inspects. A hold primes. The page ought to show the consequence of the gesture. Wonder and attention are residue of a visit, not a second document hiding the first. Kinship between notes is written as subject[mode]{parts}. The page does not guess which sentences belong together.

Spw is the move set

The same mark is readable in the source and clickable on the page. That is the ethos I am developing: interactive semantics that stay legible as text. A later pass can revise copy across the site by reading these expressions and the wonder they leave. This topic is the place that work can start.
