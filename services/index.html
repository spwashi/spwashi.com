If this needs to circulate in a business context, the short version is simple: I help teams make software, stories, and public surfaces more legible so delivery, trust, and handoff improve instead of decaying under pressure.

  • What I build directly — cover design, illustration, ebook formatting, web systems, consulting, QA. Split across creator work and systems work, with the tools page for reusable self-description and planning.
  • Who I can connect you with — an ecosystem of authors, editors, engineers, entrepreneurs, and makers. Referrals stay threaded, and the ramp is useful for someone still early in their path.

Shareable B2B Summary

Use this block when someone needs a straightforward description they can forward around a team, founder group, or investor thread.

Spwashi helps teams turn blurry work into named structure: clearer UX flows, stronger architecture notes, reusable vocabulary, more durable public surfaces, and proof artifacts that make the next decision easier to share. The useful part is not just the endpoint; it is making the in-between legible enough to trust.

What this business does

Builds readable software and media systems: scoped product work, UX-flow testing, architecture memos, creator surfaces, semantic spell recipes, and shareable proof artifacts that help a team explain itself. It also helps when the work is still in motion and needs a surface that can hold the draft without hiding it.

Why it matters long-term

Strong codebases fail when memory, timing, and communication decay. Better literacy around contracts, handoff, checkpoints, and public reasoning lowers that risk. That makes businesses more stable and leaves more room for schools, parents, and future readers to benefit from the work.

What makes the team useful

Several engineers, including a few with more than a dozen years of experience, plus author-facing and worldbuilding sensibilities that help translate technical lore into morale, alignment, and durable collaboration.

Systems Work

Custom & Hybrid Quotes

Most real work doesn't fit a pre-priced box. If your project blends a creator need with a systems need, or sits between two tiers, say so.

Send a rough sketch — a paragraph is plenty. Include what you're trying to make, what you've tried, and any constraint that matters (budget, deadline, team). I'll reply with an honest scope and a number or range within a few release cycles. If the project is a fit, I usually prefer payment up front or at the start of a phase; that keeps upbeat coordination from turning into unpaid project management.

#>cadence

Release Cadence

Release days follow a steady twice-monthly cadence: the 13th and 26th of each month. The A cycle closes on the 13th; the B cycle closes on the 26th. Twenty-four releases a year — one step more than nothing. The deeper modular rhythm powers the numericity system.

If you're starting a project and don't know how to pace it, you're welcome to borrow the rhythm. Two intentional steps a month is modest and cumulative. I ship on those days whether or not anyone is watching, which means contracts anchored to them have real landmarks.
