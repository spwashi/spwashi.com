A-cycle release · November 13

November writing, through the 30th

October 1 opened the production season, which runs to the New Year. Practice is the seed: what repeats may become a tradition. October left a costume sheet, the Inktober shelf, and a culture field. Those stay.

November is the writing in Octvember: a book for lore.land in thirteen chapters, lettered A to M, one for each operator on the lore.land table. It counts moments, not words: a written line, a scan placed with its caption, or a choice, toward 50,000 by November 30. November 1 began Chapter 0, Potential. On lore.land, Book Two, Wonder About Pi(e), opened on November 5 with its first chapter, and new chapters are set there on the 13th and 26th.

A release is a public checkpoint, not a claim that everything is finished. This page names what changed, where the proof lives, and which paths stay open.

I want my development sessions to become meditations: time to notice where my attention goes, stay with an idea, and understand why I chose one form over another. I’m also beginning a working theory of mind for this practice—asking how someone else might approach the same concept, what they might notice first, and where my explanation assumes too much. Their responses can change my understanding.

I want to strengthen my attention, reasoning, and ability to move through more specific spaces of ideas. Mathematics offers ways to name relationships, constraints, and transformations. I’d like to bring more of my own art into these explorations, grow my vocabulary, and practice sentence structures that give an idea the shape it needs. A development session can leave a drawing, a clearer sentence, or a better question alongside the code. Studies already on the shelves: the sunflower spiral, the bubble choir, and the algorithm lattice, which also opens Algorithms.

What This Funds

Time for a repeatable practice: build, publish, inspect, reroute, and keep going. Continuity includes yearly tools when the budget can hold that shape — SuperGrok, Midjourney, Codex, Claude, and more fast hours on the depiction bench — and $10–$15 top-ups when it cannot.

  • Site updates that turn an idea into a public route: a topic, a render, or a folio scan.
  • The laminated folio practice: collage, laminate, seasonal paint, and the scans that follow.
  • Local Proof Card development for asks, offers, pledges, references, and decisions.
  • Research and writing time that becomes clearer cards, lessons, methods, or services.
  • Captioned educational media, short-form videos, and grant-facing narrative work.
  • Hybridized curriculum experiments that let the learning travel farther than one room or schedule.
  • Coordination with collaborators across art, software, music, archives, learning, and care.
  • Basic stability while paid work is booked and delivered.

Support Ladder

Infrastructure backing, not charity: open software engineering, technical-proficiency equity, and accessible education require work ahead of arrival that cannot be done in hindsight. If you opened factshift.center or a public tool with a thought and a budget, each rung below names what that funding underwrites or returns. A deliverable scoped directly for your work starts as a Scope Card.

How To Back The Work

  • Claim a recurring seat. A Reader ($12/mo) or Studio ($30/mo) seat on Patreon or GitHub Sponsors funds the open tools and educational access work written up here.
  • Fund a concrete block. Pick a Signal Boost ($25–$50), Week Anchor ($150), or Season Patron ($250–$600) on the support panel—priced to match your budget to real engineering and media infrastructure.
  • Bring a thought to a Scope Card. $250 Indie, $500 Studio, or $750 Org turns a line from factshift.center or your project into a screenshotable proof card, risk note, or diagnostic memo on Services.
  • Commission a One-Close Sprint. $1,000–$2,400 ships a verified deliverable on the next 13th or 26th close, or bundle skills in Compose a bundle.
  • Live stream signals. On TikTok, send a Heart Me (Fan) or subscribe on the TikTok landing route, or interact live with lore.land's pie game during battles.
  • A card or role you can name. Fill a Local Proof Card, back the film's promo, or join as an observer, reference, working member, sponsor, or collaborator.
