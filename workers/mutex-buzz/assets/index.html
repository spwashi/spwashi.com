mutex.buzz · a lock on attention

One concept in the frame at a time.

A mutex lets one thread in and makes the rest wait. This one does that for attention: whatever holds the lock is the single thing the frame shows.

What a mutex is

A mutex, short for mutual exclusion, is a lock a program puts around something several threads might touch at once: a file, a counter, a printer. A thread acquires the lock before it touches the thing and releases it when it is done. If another thread already holds it, the newcomer waits. The code that runs while the lock is held is the critical section, and only one thread is ever inside it.

A semaphore can let several in at once; a mutex lets in exactly one, and only the one holding it can let go. One bee to a cell.

Here, the shared thing is attention. A hold is the critical section, a close is the release, and the line of anyone waiting is public.

  • holdA hold starts at a close and releases at the next: the 13th, the 26th, and the last day of the month.
  • restWhen nothing else holds it, a laminated folio does. The resting state is the work itself.
  • waitAnyone waiting lines up in public, in order.

Waiting

Nobody is waiting. The folio rests in the frame.
