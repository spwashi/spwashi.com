Supplement 2.7 · Growth · galley proof

Interest

Growth that never stops to count.

In 1683, Jacob Bernoulli asked a banker’s question. Lend one coin for a year at one hundred percent. Paid once, it becomes two. Paid half at midyear and the rest at the end, with the half already earning, it becomes 2.25.

Pay more often and it grows more, but not without limit. Monthly gives about 2.613. Daily gives about 2.715. Paid every instant, it gives 2.71828…, and that number is e.

e is what growth comes to when it never stops to count.

e

Uses

In the hand
Divide 72 by a yearly rate to find how many years it takes to double. At 6 percent, about 12.
At the table
Paying more often always helps, and past monthly it barely does: 2.613 monthly against 2.718 forever.
At work
Small gains taken often add up to a ceiling, not to infinity. Know where the ceiling is.
In the mind
Somewhere between once a year and every instant, the answer stops being a banker’s and becomes a constant.
