355/113

Six digits, six places.

About fifteen hundred years ago, the astronomer Zu Chongzhi gave two fractions for π. He called 22/7 the approximate ratio. He called 355/113 the close one.

Divide it out and it matches π to six decimal places, off by less than three ten-millionths.

No fraction with a smaller denominator comes closer. The next one that does is 52163/16604, which no one keeps anywhere.

Uses

In the hand
Write 113355, split it down the middle, and put the second half over the first. That is π to six places, with no calculator.
At the table
For roughly nine hundred years after Zu Chongzhi, no one on record had a closer value.
At work
A big jump in effort for a small gain means the good version has already been found. After 355/113, the next better fraction needs five-digit numbers.
In the mind
He reached it by hand, working with polygons of more than twelve thousand sides.
