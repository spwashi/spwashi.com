The line that led here

My public repositories go back to July 2016, the workbench and this site among them. The first, factshift, asked how content could fit a learner. Read in order, you can decide how often that question comes back.

  1. 2016–2017 — factshift, a learning tool that broke concepts into linked pages and sections so content could be tailored to a learner's needs. 60 commits.
  2. 2017–2019 — frameworks written to learn frameworks. SmJS defined entities and exported them as JSON; SmPHP read that JSON to configure its data layer; smphp-mysql wrote the queries; wanghorn was the practice app that ran on both. 722 commits between them; wanghorn alone made 86 in April 2018.
  3. 2020–2021 — the notation gets a parser. spw-client, then ts-language for building grammars, spw-editor for writing Spw, and spw-js (2021–2024), the grammar, parser and runtime. Its README says Spw came from merging a content-tagging system for educational resources with a philosophy project about analogies. react-d3 ran alongside.
  4. 2021–2023 — frontend-codebase, a React app whose to-do list was written in Spw-style brackets (132 commits in April 2023), and spw-parser (2022–2025), a from-scratch rewrite of part of spw-js.
  5. 2023–2024 — a season of small experiments: D3v7, a force-simulation playground with hotkeys (143 commits in November 2023); parser, a Spw parser in the browser; Go image and game sketches; a script that splits a layered PSD and composites its layers into short movies; tealstripesvibes.com.
  6. 2024–2026 — this site, then the workbench it mounts. spwashi.click and spwashi.work are small siblings from one weekend, February 28 to March 1, 2026.

Counts are commits by author date, as of September 2026. The repositories are public on GitHub. Spw's own starting point, a 2017 course on learning from text, is told in the website field guide.
